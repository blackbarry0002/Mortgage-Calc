## 📖 Calculator Guides

### 1️⃣ Mortgage Payment Calculator

**What It Does:** Calculates your monthly mortgage payment including taxes, insurance, and HOA fees.

**When to Use:**
- You want to know what your monthly payment will be
- You're budgeting for a home purchase
- You want to compare different loan scenarios

**How to Use:**

```
Step 1: Enter Home Price
  → Example: $350,000
  → Use the slider or type the amount
  → Tip: Adjust to see how price affects payment

Step 2: Enter Down Payment
  → Example: $70,000 (20%)
  → Percentage will auto-calculate
  → Tip: Larger down payment = lower payment

Step 3: Select Loan Term
  → Options: 15, 20, or 30 years
  → Tip: 15-year = higher payment, less interest
  → Tip: 30-year = lower payment, more interest

Step 4: Enter Interest Rate
  → Example: 6.5%
  → Use the slider or type the amount
  → Tip: Current rates vary, check your lender

Step 5 (Optional): Add Additional Costs
  → Annual Property Tax: $3,500
  → Annual Home Insurance: $1,200
  → Monthly HOA Fee: $250
  → Tip: Check these with your lender/realtor

Step 6: Click "Calculate Monthly Payment"
  → View your total payment breakdown
```

**Example Result:**
```
Home Price:               $350,000
Down Payment (20%):       $70,000
Loan Amount:              $280,000
Interest Rate:            6.5%
Term:                     30 years

Monthly P&I:              $1,770
Property Tax:             $292/month
Home Insurance:           $100/month
HOA Fee:                  $250/month
───────────────────────────────
TOTAL MONTHLY PAYMENT:     $2,412
Total Interest Paid:       $357,000
```

### 2️⃣ Home Affordability Calculator

**What It Does:** Determines the maximum home price you can afford based on your income and debt.

**When to Use:**
- You're starting your home search
- You want to know your budget
- You need to get pre-qualified
- You're comparing different financial scenarios

**How to Use:**

```
Step 1: Enter Annual Gross Income
  → Example: $100,000
  → Include all household income sources
  → Both spouses if married
  → Tip: Use last year's tax return

Step 2: Enter Monthly Debt Payments
  → Example: $500
  → Include: car loans, credit cards, student loans
  → Tip: Don't include utilities or groceries
  → Tip: Higher debt = lower home price

Step 3: Enter Down Payment
  → Example: $60,000
  → This is your available cash
  → Tip: Larger down payment = higher price

Step 4: Enter Interest Rate
  → Example: 6.5%
  → Use current market rates
  → Tip: Different rates = different affordability

Step 5: Select Loan Term
  → Options: 15, 20, or 30 years
  → 30-year is most common

Step 6: Click "Calculate Affordable Home Price"
  → See maximum price you can afford
```

**Example Result:**
```
Annual Income:            $150,000
Monthly Debt:             $300
Down Payment:             $50,000
Interest Rate:            5%
Term:                     30 years

────────────────────────────────
MAXIMUM HOME PRICE:       $701,986
Loan Amount:              $651,986
Estimated Monthly Payment: $3,500

Note: Uses 28% housing / 36% total debt limits
```

**Important:** This is an estimate. Actual approval depends on:
- Credit score
- Employment history
- Verification of income
- Final appraisal
- Lender's specific requirements

### 3️⃣ Refinance Savings Calculator

**What It Does:** Shows how much you'll save by refinancing to a lower interest rate.

**When to Use:**
- Interest rates have dropped
- You want to lower your monthly payment
- You want to pay off your mortgage faster
- You're considering a cash-out refinance

**How to Use:**

```
Step 1: Enter Current Loan Balance
  → Example: $280,000
  → Check your latest mortgage statement
  → This is the remaining amount owed

Step 2: Enter Current Interest Rate
  → Example: 7.5%
  → Check your latest mortgage statement

Step 3: Enter Current Remaining Term
  → Example: 27 years
  → How much time is left on your loan?
  → Tip: If you have 30-year loan started 3 years
           ago, remaining term is 27 years

Step 4: Enter New Interest Rate
  → Example: 4.5%
  → Get rate quotes from lenders
  → Compare multiple offers

Step 5: Select New Loan Term
  → Options: 15, 20, or 30 years
  → Note: You can change the term when refinancing

Step 6: Enter Refinance Costs
  → Example: $3,000
  → Includes: origination, appraisal, title,
             attorney, processing fees
  → Get estimate from lender

Step 7: Click "Calculate Savings"
  → See your monthly savings
  → Check break-even period
```

**Example Result:**
```
Current Loan Balance:     $280,000
Current Rate:             7.5%
Current Remaining Term:   27 years

New Rate:                 4.5%
New Term:                 30 years
Refinance Costs:          $3,000

────────────────────────────────
Current Monthly Payment:  $2,018
New Monthly Payment:      $1,419
Monthly Savings:          $599

Break-Even Period:        6 months
(When you recover your closing costs)

Total Savings Over Life:  $140,111
(After refinance costs)
```

**Should You Refinance?**
- ✅ YES if: Break-even < 5 years and you're staying
- ❌ NO if: Plan to sell within break-even period
- ❌ NO if: Rate drop is < 0.5%

### 4️⃣ Amortization Schedule

**What It Does:** Shows year-by-year breakdown of how your payments are split between principal and interest.

**When to Use:**
- You want to understand loan structure
- You're considering extra payments
- You want to see how much interest you'll pay
- You're comparing 15-year vs 30-year loans

**How to Use:**

```
Step 1: Enter Loan Amount
  → Example: $280,000
  → This is principal (what you're borrowing)

Step 2: Enter Interest Rate
  → Example: 6.5%

Step 3: Select Loan Term
  → Options: 15, 20, or 30 years

Step 4: Click "Generate Schedule"
  → View payment schedule below
```

**How to Read the Schedule:**

```
Payment #  | Payment | Principal | Interest | Balance
────────────────────────────────────────────────────
12 (Yr 1)  | $2,439  | $979      | $1,460   | $268,595
24 (Yr 2)  | $2,439  | $1,044    | $1,395   | $256,426
...
180 (Yr15) | $2,439  | $2,426    | $13      | $0

Key observations:
• Year 1: Mostly interest ($1,460) vs principal ($979)
• Year 15: Mostly principal ($2,426) vs interest ($13)
• Total paid: $2,439 × 180 = $439,020
• Total interest: $439,020 - $280,000 = $159,020
```

**What This Tells You:**
- Early payments = mostly interest
- Late payments = mostly principal
- Early payoff saves significant interest
- Extra payments reduce interest dramatically

## 🔍 FAQ - Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What's a realistic interest rate?**
A: Rates fluctuate daily. In 2024, typical ranges:
- Excellent credit: 4-5%
- Good credit: 5-6%
- Fair credit: 6-7%
- Check lender websites for current rates

**Q: Should I always put 20% down?**
A: No, options:
- 3% down: Possible, but requires PMI
- 5-10% down: Common for first-buyers
- 15-20% down: Good balance
- 25%+ down: Best loan terms

**Q: What if I can't afford 20% down?**
A: You can still buy with:
- 3-5% down payment
- Pay PMI (extra insurance cost)
- Plan to refinance when you hit 20% equity
- PMI typically costs 0.5-1.5% annually

**Q: Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better?**
A: Depends on your situation:
- 15-year: Save on interest, higher payment
- 30-year: Lower payment, more flexibility
- Choose what fits YOUR budget

**Q: How often can I refinance?**
A: No legal limit, but:
- Closing costs needed every refi
- Break-even period needed
- Wait if you'll move within break-even
- Usually worth it if rates drop 0.5%+

**Q: What's included in closing costs?**
A: Typically $3,000-8,000:
- Loan origination (lender fee)
- Appraisal
- Title search & insurance
- Attorney/legal fees
- Processing & underwriting
- Survey (sometimes)
- Ask lender for detailed estimate

**Q: Can I change my loan term when refinancing?**
A: Yes! Many options:
- Keep same term (27-year loan → 27-year refi)
- Shorten term (30-year → 15-year)
- Extend term (15-year → 30-year)
- Choose whatever works for your finances
